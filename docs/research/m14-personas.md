# M14 — Persona Pack: Alumbra Comares

Personas grounded in likely real visitors to a small independent midwife collective website in Valencia, Spain. Each persona is written to inform site decisions, not to tell a story.

---

## Persona 1 — "Primera vez, 20 semanas"

**Label:** First-time parent, early pregnancy, no prior experience with private midwifery

**Situation**
Pregnant for the first time, currently followed by her GP and the public hospital (sistema público). She has heard that a private midwife offers something more personal but has never hired one. She found Alumbra via Instagram or a friend's recommendation. She does not know the difference between a matron, an obstetrician, or a doula.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether to add private midwife accompaniment on top of her public system care, and whether this collective is the right one to contact.

**What she needs from the website**
- A clear explanation of what accompaniment actually means in practice (what happens, how often, where)
- Confirmation that private care complements rather than replaces her hospital follow-up
- Real faces and names — she wants to know who she would be speaking with
- A low-pressure first step to get in touch

**What could confuse or worry her**
- Jargon ("acompañamiento integral", "modelo de atención centrado en la mujer") without a plain-language explanation
- No pricing information at all — she will assume it is unaffordable and leave
- Being asked to commit before she understands what she is buying
- Confusing the role of the midwife with that of the hospital staff

**What would build trust**
- Short bios with real credentials and training (Isabel, Alicia, Leticia visible and named)
- A plain description of a typical session or package
- Testimonials from families in a similar situation
- Indication that the midwives work alongside, not against, the public system

**Preferred contact behavior**
WhatsApp, but only after reading enough to feel comfortable. She will not call a phone number cold. She wants to feel she already "knows" them a little before reaching out.

## Persona 2 — "Comparando opciones, 28 semanas"

**Label:** Parent actively comparing two or three private midwife options in Valencia

**Situation**
26–32 weeks pregnant, already decided she wants a private midwife, now choosing between two or three options she has found online. She is methodical. She has read websites, looked at Instagram, and may have already had one intro call elsewhere. She has a budget range in mind.

**What she is trying to decide**
Which collective offers the best fit for her values, practical availability, and price. She is judging professionalism and warmth in equal measure.

**What she needs from the website**
- Service descriptions clear enough to compare (what is included, duration, number of visits)
- Price indication or at minimum a signal about the price range
- Evidence of the team's qualifications and specialisms
- A sense of how the collective actually works — three midwives, how is continuity managed?

**What could confuse or worry her**
- No pricing, no package structure, nothing to compare
- Vague service names with no specifics
- No indication of whether all three midwives attend birth or just one
- A "contact us to find out more" CTA with no other information — she will move on

**What would build trust**
- Individual midwife profiles with distinct credentials (e.g. Isabel's UK training, Alicia's lactation specialisation, Leticia's specific areas)
- An FAQ or "how we work" section that explains continuity of care
- Testimonials that mention specific services
- A clear, non-pushy first contact option

**Preferred contact behavior**
Intro call or brief WhatsApp exchange to ask two or three specific questions. She is ready to act quickly if the site gives her enough to make a decision.

## Persona 3 — "Co-padre curioso, 32 semanas"

**Label:** Co-parent (partner/father) researching independently, moderate digital confidence

**Situation**
The pregnant partner has heard about Alumbra. The co-parent has been asked to look into it, or is doing so independently to understand what is being considered. He is not sceptical, but he is practical. He wants to understand what the family would be paying for and whether it is worth it.

**What he is trying to decide**
Whether the service is credible, what his role would be in the process, and what the cost looks like relative to what is included.

**What he needs from the website**
- Plain-language service descriptions written for someone outside the maternal care bubble
- A clear indication that partners are included and welcome, not an afterthought
- Credentials and professional legitimacy (registrations, training, insurance if mentioned)
- A sense of what birth support actually involves for the co-parent's role

**What could confuse or worry him**
- Copy that addresses only "mamás" — he will feel excluded and lose interest
- No indication of what is covered by private midwifery vs the public hospital
- Heavily emotional language without any practical information alongside it

**What would build trust**
- Mention of partner workshops or co-parent involvement in service descriptions
- Real credentials visible on the team page
- Calm, clear language — not cold, but not exclusively emotional

**Preferred contact behavior**
He will share the website with his partner and suggest she contact them, or he may send a brief WhatsApp question. He will not fill in a form.

## Persona 4 — "Visita por recomendación, postparto reciente"

**Label:** Referred visitor, baby recently born, seeking postpartum or lactation support

**Situation**
Two to six weeks postpartum. A friend, her midwife at the hospital, or her paediatrician has mentioned Alumbra by name. She is tired, possibly struggling with feeding or recovery, and is not browsing — she is looking for confirmation that this is the right contact and then she wants to reach them immediately.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether Alumbra offers what she needs right now (postpartum visit, lactation consultation) and how quickly she can get an appointment.

**What she needs from the website**
- Postpartum and lactation services visible quickly — not buried under pregnancy content
- A direct, fast contact route (WhatsApp button prominent)
- An indication of response time or availability
- Confirmation that home visits are available

**What could confuse or worry her**
- A site that feels exclusively focused on pregnancy, with postpartum as a footnote
- No WhatsApp button or only a form (she does not have energy to fill in a form)
- No mention of home visits — she cannot easily leave the house
- Unclear whether she needs to be an existing client to book a postpartum session

**What would build trust**
- Explicit mention of postpartum and lactation as first-class services
- A clear home visit offer
- A short sentence confirming new enquiries are welcome (not just returning clients)
- Quick response — if she sends a WhatsApp and hears back within a few hours, trust is built

**Preferred contact behavior**
WhatsApp, now. She will not browse further once she has found a contact button.

## Persona 5 — "Asistente a taller, sin embarazo actual"

**Label:** Event-curious visitor, not currently pregnant, interested in workshops or community

**Situation**
Has seen an Instagram post or been told about a workshop — birth preparation, lactation, or postpartum circle. May be planning a pregnancy, may be supporting someone else, may simply be interested in the topic. She is not in an urgent decision mode.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether to attend an event or sign up to hear about future ones. Possibly forming a longer-term impression of the collective for when she is pregnant.

**What she needs from the website**
- A clear events or workshops section that is easy to find
- Date, format, location, and cost for upcoming events
- A way to stay informed (newsletter or Instagram follow prompt) without having to commit
- A warm sense of who these midwives are

**What could confuse or worry her**
- No events section, or an events section that says "coming soon" with nothing to click
- Being pushed toward a contact form designed for pregnant clients
- No way to stay in touch without committing to a service inquiry

**What would build trust**
- A live or recently updated events listing
- A low-friction sign-up or follow option (Instagram link, newsletter)
- Copy that makes clear events are open to non-clients

**Preferred contact behavior**
Instagram DM or a lightweight event sign-up. She will not send a WhatsApp to a number she does not know.

## Persona 6 — "Mamá con poca confianza digital, referida por su ginecólogo"

**Label:** Low digital confidence, older first-time parent or rural visitor, referred by a professional

**Situation**
35–42 years old, referred by her gynaecologist or midwife at the public hospital. Has limited experience navigating health websites. Uses her phone primarily for WhatsApp and Instagram, not forms or web browsing. May be accessing the site on a small phone screen with poor connectivity.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether to call or message. She has been told to contact Alumbra — she just needs to find a number or a button.

**What she needs from the website**
- A phone number and WhatsApp button immediately visible, not hidden in a contact page
- Simple language, short sentences, no jargon
- A sense of warmth — she is worried about being judged or treated impersonally
- The site to work on a mid-range Android phone with a 4G connection

**What could confuse or worry her**
- A long-scrolling single page with dense text before any contact information
- Forms with many required fields
- Language that assumes familiarity with private healthcare concepts
- Any sign that this is only for a certain type of person (highly educated, wealthy, alternative)

**What would build trust**
- A phone number she can tap to call
- Friendly, non-clinical language
- Faces and names — she wants to know she is contacting a real person
- Short, direct descriptions of what happens when you get in touch

**Preferred contact behavior**
Phone call or WhatsApp tap-to-message. She will not fill in a form.

## Persona 7 — "Parto en casa, primera vez"

**Label:** First-time parent actively considering home birth, no prior experience

**Situation**
12–28 weeks pregnant, has been reading about home birth and feels drawn to it, but has never done this before. Does not fully understand the legal and practical landscape in Spain — home birth is legal but rarely supported by the public system. Found Alumbra while searching for matronas who attend home births in Valencia.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether Alumbra supports home birth, and if so, whether this team has enough experience for her to trust them with something she knows is outside the norm.

**What she needs from the website**
- Clear confirmation that home birth is offered (or not)
- Information about the team's home birth experience and number of births attended
- What happens in a transfer scenario — she knows the risk perception and wants transparency
- Reassurance that her choice won't be judged

**What could confuse or worry her**
- No mention of home birth at all — she'll assume they don't offer it
- Vague "accompaniment" language that could mean anything
- No acknowledgment of transfer protocol or safety framework
- Content that hedges excessively or frames home birth as unusual

**What would build trust**
- Explicit mention of home birth under services, with honest scope
- Team bios that include home birth experience or number of attended births
- A calm, non-defensive explanation of safety and transfer protocols
- Testimonials from families who had home births with the team

**Preferred contact behavior**
WhatsApp — she has specific questions and wants a real conversation, not a form.

## Persona 8 — "Parto en casa, repite"

**Label:** Second-time parent, had a home birth before, evaluating whether to use this team

**Situation**
Already pregnant, had a positive home birth with a different midwife or in another city. Moving to Valencia or switching providers. She knows exactly what good home birth care looks like and has high standards. She will notice gaps quickly.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether this team is experienced enough and a good fit for her existing expectations. She is comparing Alumbra against her previous experience, not against a hospital.

**What she needs from the website**
- Home birth listed clearly as a service
- Evidence of team experience (number of births attended, training, continuity of care model)
- Honest scope of what is and isn't covered
- A signal that the team won't over-medicalise or try to redirect her to hospital

**What could confuse or worry her**
- Generic language that doesn't specifically address birth attendance
- No indication of how continuity works across the three midwives
- Credentials that are hospital-focused without acknowledging community or home birth context

**What would build trust**
- Specific mention of home birth in team bios or service cards
- Explicit continuity of care explanation (who attends the birth)
- Professional registration and home birth experience visible

**Preferred contact behavior**
Direct WhatsApp or phone call. She is ready to book an intro conversation immediately.

## Persona 9 — "Parto anterior traumático, busca otra experiencia"

**Label:** Parent who experienced an over-interventionist hospital birth and is seeking something different

**Situation**
Has at least one prior birth — often a caesarean or heavily medicated birth she felt pressured into. May be considering a VBAC (vaginal birth after caesarean). She is likely carrying emotional weight from the previous experience and is looking for a team that will respect her autonomy and decisions.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether Alumbra's values genuinely align with birth autonomy and minimal intervention, or whether this is marketing language. She is cautious and has been let down before.

**What she needs from the website**
- Language that explicitly acknowledges birth autonomy and the right to informed choice
- Evidence that the team advocates for the mother, including in hospital settings
- Clear explanation of the advocacy/accompaniment role during labour
- No dismissal of her previous experience through over-positive framing

**What could confuse or worry her**
- Generic wellness language that feels performative
- No mention of advocacy during hospital birth or birth plan support
- Content that romanticises birth without acknowledging that it can be difficult
- Anything that suggests the team will defer to hospital staff over her wishes

**What would build trust**
- Explicit language about accompanying and advocating for mothers during birth, including in hospital
- Mention of birth plan preparation as a service
- Testimonials from mothers who felt heard and supported, especially those with difficult histories
- Calm, grounded tone — not evangelical about birth, but clearly values-led

**Preferred contact behavior**
WhatsApp with a few direct questions before any conversation. She needs to feel safe before she opens up.

## Persona 10 — "Expat anglófona, busca atención en inglés"

**Label:** English-speaking expat (UK, Ireland, US, Australia, or non-Spanish European) seeking English-language care

**Situation**
Living in Valencia, pregnant, with limited Spanish. May have conversational Spanish but lacks the vocabulary for medical conversations. Actively searching for English-speaking health providers. Found Alumbra through an expat group, Facebook community, or Google.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether any of the three midwives speak English well enough for medical conversations, and whether the service is structured for international clients.

**What she needs from the website**
- A clear signal that English is spoken — ideally listed on team profiles
- Confidence that the midwife can explain medical concepts in English
- Basic orientation to how Spanish private midwifery works compared to NHS or her home country model
- A direct contact route

**What could confuse or worry her**
- A fully Spanish-only site with no English fallback signal — she will assume no English is spoken
- Credentials listed without explaining their Spanish equivalents ("Matrona" — she may not know this is a registered midwife)
- References to the Spanish sistema público she doesn't fully understand

**What would build trust**
- "Hablamos inglés" or equivalent in team bios or contact section
- A future English version of the site (even if coming soon)
- Isabel's UK training background mentioned explicitly — directly relevant here

**Preferred contact behavior**
WhatsApp in English. She will test immediately by sending a message in English to see if they respond in kind.

## Persona 11 — "Expat con expectativas de otro sistema sanitario"

**Label:** Expat from a midwife-led birth culture (Netherlands, Germany, Scandinavia, UK) confused by Spain's hospital-centric model

**Situation**
Pregnant and living in Valencia, from a country where independent midwives and home birth are standard and sometimes publicly funded. Frustrated or confused by the Spanish system, which routes everything through hospitals. May have had a disappointing first encounter with the sistema público. Expects continuity of care, a named midwife, and home birth as a realistic option.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether Alumbra offers something close to what she experienced back home, and whether it can bridge the gap between her expectations and Spanish healthcare norms.

**What she needs from the website**
- Clear explanation of how private midwifery in Spain works and what it covers
- Confirmation that home birth is an option (she will assume it is — in her home system it's standard)
- An honest account of where Alumbra works within the Spanish system vs independently
- Language competence signal

**What could confuse or worry her**
- Content that assumes she understands the sistema público
- No mention of home birth or only vague accompaniment language
- The three-midwife model with no explanation of continuity — at home she had one named midwife for everything

**What would build trust**
- Clear service descriptions with explicit scope (prenatal, birth, postpartum)
- Explicit mention of home birth availability
- A tone that validates her expectations without dismissing Spanish realities
- English content signal

**Preferred contact behavior**
WhatsApp. She may initially write in English or her home language to test before switching to Spanish.

## Persona 12 — "Familiar influyente — madre o suegra" (suggested)

**Label:** Influential family member (mother or mother-in-law) who may support or block the decision

**Situation**
The pregnant person has mentioned considering a private midwife or home birth. The family member has been sent the website or found it herself. She may be sceptical — in her generation, hospital birth was the norm and private midwifery was either unknown or associated with risk. Her opinion carries weight in the family.

**What she is trying to decide**
Whether this is a serious, professional service or something "alternative" she should discourage. She is assessing legitimacy, not values.

**What she needs from the website**
- Professional credentials visible and prominent — real qualifications, registrations, experience
- Language that sounds grounded and credible, not evangelical
- Clear signals that the service works alongside the public hospital, not against it
- Confirmation that these are trained professionals, not wellness influencers

**What could confuse or worry her**
- Heavily spiritual or alternative-lifestyle language
- No visible credentials or professional registrations
- Content that implies the hospital is dangerous or to be avoided
- No mention of what happens in an emergency or when a transfer is needed

**What would build trust**
- Named midwives with visible training and registration
- Clear statement that the service complements, not replaces, hospital care
- Testimonials from families (not just mothers — partners or family members quoted)
- A calm, professional tone readable as credible to a sceptical reader

**Preferred contact behavior**
She will not contact them. But a credible site changes the conversation she has with her daughter or daughter-in-law.

## Persona 13 — "Profesional sanitario derivante" (suggested)

**Label:** Gynaecologist, paediatrician, or hospital midwife considering recommending Alumbra to patients

**Situation**
A healthcare professional who has heard about Alumbra from a patient or colleague. They visit the site to assess whether this is a service they can safely recommend. Not emotionally invested — evaluating professional competence, scope of practice, and liability.

**What they are trying to decide**
Whether this collective is competent, insured, within appropriate scope, and worth a professional recommendation.

**What they need from the website**
- Visible professional registrations (número de colegiada or equivalent)
- Scope of practice clearly defined — what the team does and does not do
- Indication of professional insurance
- Clear transfer and emergency protocol
- Contact route for professional enquiries

**What could confuse or worry them**
- No credentials or registration numbers visible
- Language that implies the team substitutes for medical care
- No mention of hospital transfer protocol
- Anything that could expose them to liability if a patient has a bad outcome after their referral

**What would build trust**
- Professional registration numbers visible on team profiles (directly relevant to M18)
- An explicit scope-of-care statement
- Insurance status mentioned
- Language that positions them as complementary to, not in competition with, clinical care

**Preferred contact behavior**
Email or phone call. They will not use WhatsApp for a professional enquiry.

## Notes on Coverage

These thirteen personas collectively cover:

- First-time parent in early pregnancy (P1)
- Parent actively comparing options (P2)
- Co-parent / partner perspective (P3)
- Postpartum / referred urgency (P4)
- Event-curious / community visitor (P5)
- Low-digital-confidence / referred visitor (P6)
- First-time home birth consideration (P7)
- Repeat home birth — high-standards evaluator (P8)
- Traumatic prior birth — autonomy-seeking (P9)
- English-speaking expat (P10)
- Expat from midwife-led system, expectation mismatch (P11)
- Influential sceptical family member (P12)
- Professional referrer — clinical credibility check (P13)

Both original open questions from the milestone are addressed: co-parent via P3, low digital confidence via P6. P12 and P13 are additions suggested beyond the initial brief — both have direct implications for site copy, credential visibility, and tone decisions.
