The core question behind the synthesis: what should a warm, Mediterranean, non-corporate, clinically credible midwife collective website feel like, say, include and avoid?

# Executive summary

The strongest pattern across the research is not a particular template or platform. It is a balance: competent tenderness. Families need to feel that Alumbra is safe, qualified and organised, but they also need to feel held, seen and not processed through a maternity sales funnel with scented candles thrown in for atmosphere.

- **Lead with relationship and continuity.** The best sites promise a known midwife or small known team, not anonymous services. This is especially powerful for independent care, where families are paying for presence, judgement and emotional safety.

- **Organise around the family journey.** Pregnancy, birth, postpartum and lactation are easier for visitors than professional taxonomies. Families arrive with a moment in life, not a sitemap.

- **Make trust visible in layers.** Use real photos, named bios, registration numbers, credentials, insurance, safety protocols, testimonials, FAQs and practical role boundaries. No single signal is enough.

- **Make first contact low-friction.** In Spain, WhatsApp Business should be a primary route, supported by phone, email and a simple form. A free introductory call or meeting is one of the strongest conversion and trust mechanisms.

- **Be transparent enough to reduce anxiety.** Baseline pricing, package logic, travel zones, what is included and what happens in transfers all reduce the silent friction that stops families from enquiring.

- **Avoid premature complexity.** A first site should not behave like an academy, directory, shop, blog network and booking engine all at once. A beautiful 5-6 page site beats a sprawling half-finished cathedral of good intentions.

# 1. Strategic positioning canvas

Alumbra should occupy the space between clinical service, village support and Mediterranean home. The positioning should be relational rather than transactional, evidence-led rather than institutional, and warm without drifting into vague mysticism.

| **Dimension**         | **Recommended stance**                                                                                                                                              | **Why it matters**                                                                            |
|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Core promise          | A small collective of qualified comares/midwives accompanying pregnancy, birth, postpartum and lactation with continuity, calm and clinical judgement.              | Independent care is bought for relationship and trust, not just tasks.                        |
| Emotional territory   | Warm, grounded, local, calm, family-centred, respectful of physiology and autonomy.                                                                                 | This attracts families seeking a human alternative without making the site feel anti-medical. |
| Credibility territory | Visible registration, experience, insurance, evidence, protocols, role boundaries and transfer logic.                                                               | Spain is a regulated context; trust must be explicit, not assumed.                            |
| Differentiator        | Competent tenderness in Valencia: real people, real local care, clear services, low-friction contact.                                                               | A memorable middle ground between corporate clinic and airy wellness fog.                     |
| Audience              | Pregnant women, partners and families looking for independent support before, during and after birth; also expat/international families if language support exists. | The site must reassure both the mother and the family system around her.                      |

# 2. Brand principles to guide the website

## Human before institutional

Use real names, faces, domestic or natural light photography, and specific stories. Avoid stock-photo clinic polish. The family should feel they are meeting people, not an organisation trying to sound like people.

**Useful references:** Best references: Maternalmente, Conmatronas, Womb & Bloom, Midwives 101.

## Evidence before ideology

Physiology, autonomy and respectful birth should be central, but the site should anchor these ideas in training, protocols and clear boundaries. Philosophy should invite; it should not require the visitor to pass a spiritual vocabulary exam.

**Useful references:** Best references: Antonia Rojo, Elena Pajuelo, Casa Laietania, Le CALM.

## Collective, not celebrity

The reports repeatedly warn against the solo-founder trap. A collective needs a consistent we, shared values, shared standards and individual practitioner depth inside that larger identity.

**Useful references:** Best references: Magale Etxea, Maternalmente, Womb & Bloom, Melbourne Midwifery Collective.

## Smallness as a feature

A small practice should not apologise for being small. It should make smallness mean more time, more continuity, fewer unknowns and less conveyor-belt care.

**Useful references:** Best references: Midwives 101, Conmatronas, Clair de Lune.

## Clarity is care

Visitors should know what is offered, who it is for, where it happens, what it includes, when to enquire, and how to ask a question. Clarity is not less warm. It is often the warmest thing on the page.

**Useful references:** Best references: Elena Pajuelo, My Independent Midwife, Casa Laietania, Geboortecentrum Amsterdam.

# 3. Information architecture: the recommended shape

The best-fit structure is hybrid: one generous homepage that tells the story and routes the visitor, supported by a small number of depth pages. A pure one-page site can feel thin; a large multi-page site can feel like homework. Hybrid gives warmth plus practicality.

| **Page / section**     | **Role**                      | **What it should contain**                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|------------------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Home                   | Emotional landing and routing | Hero with Valencia/local identity, 3-line promise, primary WhatsApp CTA, short philosophy, service pathways, trust markers, testimonials preview, “how we work” summary.                                     |
| Services               | Clarity and conversion        | Pregnancy, birth support/home birth, postpartum, lactation and groups/classes. For each: who it is for, what is included, where it happens, duration, lead practitioner(s), starting price or package logic. |
| Team / Nosotras        | Trust through faces           | Real photos, names, registration numbers, credentials, languages, specialisms, 3-part bios: why this work, clinical background, how they care. Include shared collective origin story.                       |
| How we work / Safety   | Reassurance and boundaries    | Eligibility criteria where relevant, transfer protocols, public/private care relationship, out-of-hours model, travel zone, what to expect after first contact. Could be a page or a major section.          |
| Testimonials / Stories | Social proof                  | Short quotes plus longer birth/postpartum stories. Rotate across services and practitioners so the collective identity is reinforced.                                                                        |
| FAQs                   | Anxiety reduction             | Pricing, timing, safety, home birth, hospital support, lactation, postpartum visits, role boundaries, what happens in urgent situations, languages, travel zones.                                            |
| Contact                | Effortless entry              | WhatsApp Business, phone, email, form, Instagram, location or service area, free introductory meeting CTA. Repeat key channels in footer.                                                                    |

## Homepage narrative sequence

1.  **Hero:** who you are, where you work, and the feeling of care in one glance.

2.  **Pathways:** Pregnancy, Birth, Postpartum, Lactation, Groups - each with a simple “see how we can help” link.

3.  **Collective philosophy:** a concise statement about physiology, autonomy, respectful care and safety.

4.  **Meet the comares:** photos and one-line descriptors, linking to fuller bios.

5.  **How the first conversation works:** free intro call, WhatsApp, or form; remove the fear of commitment.

6.  **Trust layer:** colegiadas, insurance, experience, affiliations, reviews/testimonials and practical safety boundaries.

7.  **Final CTA:** warm, specific and direct: “Cuéntanos en qué momento estás y te orientamos”.

# 4. Service architecture: what families should understand quickly

The research strongly supports organising services by life stage and decision level. Families should not have to know whether they need “accompaniment”, “clinical consultation”, “continuity package”, “voucher” or “workshop”. They should be able to start with: I am pregnant, I am thinking about birth, I have just had a baby, feeding is hard, or I need community.

| **Service family**                  | **Best-practice presentation**                                                                                                                                                            | **Relevant reference patterns**                                                                                                   |
|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Pregnancy accompaniment             | Prenatal visits, birth planning, emotional preparation, partner role, informed choices, warning signs, connection to public system. Offer one-off sessions and bundles.                   | Elena Pajuelo packages; Maternalmente pregnancy pathway; Antonia Rojo continuity model.                                           |
| Birth support / home labour support | A middle-ground offer such as dilatation at home before hospital transfer. Explain what the midwife does, when she comes, where the support ends, and how this complements hospital care. | Cristina Perez / “Dilatacion en Casa” pattern; My Independent Midwife hospital birth support; Antonia Rojo homebirth explanation. |
| Home birth care                     | Map the full journey: initial consultation, eligibility, visits, home assessment, week 37-42 on-call window, birth team, equipment, transfer plan, postpartum follow-up.                  | Laura Sola; Le CALM; Casa Laietania; Antonia Rojo.                                                                                |
| Postpartum recovery                 | Home visits for maternal recovery, newborn checks, feeding support, emotional wellbeing and practical orientation. Package into 2-session or 4-session bundles.                           | Elena Pajuelo home visits; Grace Midwifery postpartum packages; Maternalmente postpartum community.                               |
| Lactation / feeding                 | Individual consultations with duration, what is assessed, follow-up window and home/clinic/online options. If IBCLC exists, say so visibly.                                               | Elena Pajuelo IBCLC; Maria Gomez-Cabrero; NiuMA; Conmatronas.                                                                     |
| Groups and workshops                | Use community-led offers sparingly at launch: birth preparation, postpartum circle, first aid, baby care, partner workshops, AIPAP or movement if genuinely offered.                      | Maternalmente “La Guarida”; East River free events; Geboortecentrum info evenings.                                                |

## Package logic that works

- **Baseline transparency.** Show starting prices or clear package ranges. When pricing varies, explain why: travel, timing, number of visits, two-midwife attendance, on-call period, or clinical complexity.

- **Bonos / vouchers.** Flexible consultation bundles are repeatedly useful because needs change across pregnancy and postpartum. They also avoid making every visit feel like a new purchase decision.

- **Geographical tiers.** For home visits, separate Valencia city, metropolitan area and further travel zones. This prevents awkwardness later and makes the service feel professionally managed.

- **Free first meeting.** A free 30-60 minute introductory call or meeting appears as a high-trust pattern across many strong sites. It is particularly valuable for a new collective.

- **Do not turn care into a shop too early.** Visible prices are good; checkout-first UX for intimate services is risky. For birth work, conversation-first usually feels better than “Add to basket”.

# 5. Trust architecture: the non-negotiables

Trust should be built in layers. A visitor may not consciously inspect every layer, but the combined effect is what makes the site feel safe enough to contact.

| **Trust layer**         | **What to include**                                                                                                                            | **Why it works**                                                                                                 |
|-------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| People                  | Real photos, named bios, personal stories, languages, specialisms and care style.                                                              | Birth work is relational. Visitors need to know who may be in their home or with them during vulnerable moments. |
| Professional legitimacy | Colegiada numbers, title boundaries, training, certifications, affiliations, insurance and health registration where applicable.               | In Spain, matrona is a regulated professional identity. Visible legitimacy reduces doubt.                        |
| Experience              | Years, number of families accompanied, types of care, hospital/community background, special cases only if relevant and responsibly described. | Specific experience is more convincing than broad adjectives like “expert”.                                      |
| Safety and boundaries   | Eligibility criteria, transfer plan, urgent contact model, what is and is not covered, public-system relationship.                             | Families considering independent care often worry about safety but may not know how to ask.                      |
| Social proof            | Testimonials, Google reviews, birth stories, thank-you notes, service-specific quotes.                                                         | Testimonials convert abstract care into a believable felt experience.                                            |
| Practical transparency  | Pricing, travel zones, session length, follow-up window, response expectations, what happens after enquiry.                                    | Practical clarity lowers anxiety and prevents mismatched expectations.                                           |

## Safety language: warm but not romanticised

The strongest safety pages do not sound defensive. They sound calm. A good structure is: who the service is for, what makes it safe, what you monitor, when you recommend transfer or referral, and what the family can expect emotionally and practically. The key is to make safety part of care, not an awkward appendix hidden behind the footer like a suspicious suitcase.

# 6. Contact and conversion: make the first step feel small

- **Primary CTA: WhatsApp Business.** Use a dedicated Alumbra number, not personal phones. Suggested label: “¿Tienes dudas? Escríbenos y te orientamos”.

- **Secondary CTA: free intro call or meeting.** This should be positioned as a way to understand the family’s moment and see whether the fit feels right.

- **Contact form: short and reassuring.** Ask for name, weeks pregnant or baby age, area, service interest, preferred contact channel, and one open text field. Avoid making the first contact feel like an insurance claim.

- **Repeat CTAs.** Every service page should end with the same warm next step: talk to us, ask a question, or book an introductory conversation.

- **Clarify urgency.** If the service is not for emergencies, say so clearly and kindly. If there is out-of-hours support for active clients, explain when it applies.

# 7. Content and SEO strategy

The goal is not to publish lots of content. The goal is to answer high-intent questions better than thin competitor pages. A small number of rich pages can outperform a blog that quietly dies after three posts and one cheerful “Hello world”.

| **Area**    | **Recommendation**                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Local SEO   | Create and maintain Google Business Profile. Optimise pages for Valencia and surrounding areas. Include address/service area, phone, photos, reviews and categories.                                                |
| Service SEO | Give each major service its own rich section or page: matrona Valencia, matrona privada Valencia, parto en casa Valencia, acompañamiento embarazo Valencia, posparto Valencia, lactancia Valencia.                  |
| Language    | Launch Spanish first. Add Valencian/Catalan if authentic to the collective. Add English if international families are genuinely part of the expected audience. Do not create three half-finished language versions. |
| FAQs        | Use FAQ content to target real anxiety-based queries: safety, price, when to call, home birth criteria, hospital birth support, breastfeeding help, postpartum visits.                                              |
| Blog        | Optional later. Publish only if there is a maintainable cadence and a clear owner. One excellent article per month beats twelve thin articles in a launch sprint.                                                   |

# 8. Visual identity and tone of voice

| **Element** | **Best-practice direction**                                                                      | **Avoid**                                                                                                         |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Colour      | Terracotta, sand, cream, olive, linen and soft Mediterranean earth tones.                        | Sterile blue-white clinic palette; excessive wellness pink; very dark mystical palettes.                          |
| Photography | Real team photos, hands, homes, Valencia light, calm domestic settings, families if consented.   | Generic pregnancy stock photos, perfect model families, hospital-corridor imagery unless intentionally practical. |
| Typography  | Readable humanist sans for body, warm serif or soft display face for headings if used sparingly. | Overly corporate type, tiny elegant fonts that fail on mobile.                                                    |
| Voice       | Calm, plain, evidence-led, tender, direct, occasionally gently playful.                          | Luxury sales language, fear-based copy, vague spiritual intensity, jargon-heavy clinical prose.                   |
| Metaphor    | Home, light, accompaniment, village, rhythm, care, continuity.                                   | Overcooked goddess/temple language unless the collective truly wants narrow positioning.                          |

# 9. Pitfalls to avoid

8.  **Solo-voice trap.** Do not let a collective read as one charismatic founder plus helpers. Use “we”, shared standards and individual bios.

9.  **Ideology before clarity.** Values matter, but visitors first need to understand what you do, whether it is safe, and how to contact you.

10. **Pricing opacity.** Some families will assume “if I have to ask, I cannot afford it”. Give ranges, starting prices or package logic.

11. **Role ambiguity.** If doulas, psychologists, lactation consultants or physiotherapists appear, explain role boundaries. This protects trust and legal clarity.

12. **Menu sprawl.** Avoid launching with a large academy, shop, blog, resource library, collaborator directory and newsletter unless each has a real owner.

13. **Transactional UX.** Care can have prices without feeling like e-commerce. Initial contact should remain human.

14. **Neglected content.** A stale blog, broken social embed or outdated course calendar damages trust more than no blog at all.

15. **Too institutional or too airy.** The sweet spot is warm specificity: not hospital bureaucracy, not mystical fog machine.

# 12. Decisions to make before writing the final site copy

- What exact services will Alumbra offer at launch, and which are future-only?

- Will the brand use comares, matronas, or both? If both, when and why?

- What are the professional credentials, registration numbers, insurance details and affiliations that can be shown publicly?

- What geographic area will be covered for home visits, and will travel fees vary by zone?

- What level of pricing transparency is acceptable: exact prices, starting prices, package ranges or “from” prices?

- Is home birth offered directly, or is the initial focus pregnancy, hospital support, postpartum and lactation?

- What is the out-of-hours model for active clients, and how should it be described safely?

- Which languages can the collective genuinely support?

- Who owns content maintenance after launch, so the site does not become a tiny museum of abandoned enthusiasm?

# Final recommendation

Build a Spanish-first hybrid website that feels like a calm conversation with a highly qualified small team. The first version should prioritise: visible comares, clear services, transparent enough pricing, safety clarity, testimonials, WhatsApp-first contact, and a free first conversation. The visual world should be Mediterranean and real: warm light, human faces, earth colours and practical language.

In simple terms: make the site feel like the family has found people who will answer the phone, know what they are doing, and treat birth and postpartum as human life rather than a workflow. That is the standard to aspire to.
